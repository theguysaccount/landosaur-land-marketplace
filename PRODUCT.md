# PRODUCT

**User.** Land buyers the big portals underserve: neighbours who want the lot next door, homesteaders, small
builders, gardeners and community groups, and investors comparing tax-sale and state land.

**Painful job.** Government land is scattered across city spreadsheets, state auction grids and agency pages, each
with its own terms and none on a map. Buyers can't see what's available near them, when bidding closes, or what it
will really cost.

**Promise.** Every official land offering we can read, on one map, with the agency's facts, the date they were
read, a link to buy through the agency, and a worksheet for the all-in cost.

**Wedge.** "New government land listings" and "closing soon" pages with per-state Atom feeds: alerts without email
or accounts, and pages the original application doesn't have.

**Why now.** Cities and states publish inventories as open data and GIS services; a scheduled job can keep a
national view current at no cost.

**Activation metric.** A visitor opens an agency listing from a Landosaur listing page (instrumented once
`LANDO_GOATCOUNTER` is set). Proxy until then: feed subscriptions are not countable; IndexNow/Bing impressions on
/new/ and /closing-soon/.

**First revenue event.** A paid parcel report ordered from a listing page (`LANDO_REPORT_URL`, off Pages).

**What v1 deliberately won't do.** Sell, broker or hold land; give legal, tax, title or investment advice; appraise;
copy commercial portals; publish owner names or photos; take payments on GitHub Pages.
