# Cash price, down payment and opening bid

The smallest number on a land advertisement may describe an entry payment rather than the property’s price. Before sorting a shortlist from cheapest to most expensive, identify the obligation represented by every number. Otherwise, a $2,000 down payment can appear beside a $20,000 cash purchase as though they were equivalent offers.

## Give each number a name

A cash asking price is the seller’s stated price for the offered interest on the stated cash terms. A down payment is an initial contribution toward a larger financed purchase. A monthly payment is a recurring obligation. An opening bid is where an auction may start, not necessarily the amount required to win. A bid deposit may be security for performance rather than part of the eventual advertised price.

Sale procedures vary. Alaska’s state land program, for example, distinguishes lump-sum purchases from sale contracts and describes a separate over-the-counter route for parcels not sold at auction. Those are program-specific terms, not nationwide transaction rules. [Alaska DNR land-sales FAQ](https://dnr.alaska.gov/mlw/landsales/info/faq/).

Request a complete written price and payment schedule before comparing offers. Keep seller fees, closing charges, taxes and improvements outside the headline price so you can see what has and has not been included.

## Add the entire schedule

Use a simple cash-outlay worksheet: initial payment, number and size of installments, final payment, mandatory fees and other buyer-paid costs. Separately record the purchase price, financed principal, stated interest rate, payment frequency and whether taxes or insurance are collected with payments. They answer different questions.

**Hypothetical example:** an offer requires $5,000 down, 60 payments of $500 and a final $20,000 balloon. Scheduled payments total $55,000 before other costs. That is not a $5,000 property, and the $55,000 total is not automatically its cash-equivalent value. Timing and the agreed financing terms matter.

A balloon leaves a larger payment due later. CFPB explains that refinancing may not be available if a borrower’s circumstances or property value deteriorate. Its consumer mortgage explanation is useful for understanding the payment risk; application of particular mortgage protections to a land transaction requires separate review. [CFPB balloon payment explanation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-balloon-payment-when-is-one-allowed-en-104/).

## Compare offers at the same level

- Compare complete cash asking prices with other complete cash asking prices.
- Compare financed offers using full schedules, deed timing and default terms.
- Model deposits as cash needed at the actual deadline, including any refund conditions.
- Treat auction fees according to the specific terms: confirm whether they are added to the accepted bid.
- Label missing terms as unknown rather than filling them with typical rates or assumed fees.

Before paying, reconcile the advertisement, contract and settlement instructions. A screenshot of the payment headline is useful evidence of an offer, but it is not the entire agreement.
