# Buy conservation land with the restrictions in view

A conservation easement can protect the qualities that make a property attractive while limiting uses a future owner might otherwise expect. The recorded instrument is central to the asset you are buying. Read its permissions, restrictions and stewardship obligations before comparing the property with unrestricted land.

## Obtain the complete easement file

Request the recorded easement, amendments, maps, baseline documentation, management plans and relevant correspondence. Identify the holder and ask what information it can provide about compliance, existing approvals and the process for future requests. Confirm the land and rights affected rather than assuming the restriction covers either every acre or only the visually sensitive area.

USDA NRCS’s Agricultural Land Easements program illustrates one conservation framework: protection of agricultural uses and related conservation values through participating entities and program requirements. It is one program, not a definition of every privately negotiated easement. [NRCS agricultural land easements](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/programs-initiatives/ale-agricultural-land-easements).

## Test your actual use against the document

Make a list of intended activities: residence, accessory buildings, subdivision, farming, timber work, habitat management, trails, utilities, commercial activity and future transfer. For each, ask qualified counsel to identify whether it is permitted, prohibited, limited to a designated area or subject to approval.

Do not rely on a general description such as “agricultural use allowed.” The document may distinguish different agricultural structures, practices or intensities. An existing building does not automatically establish permission for a larger replacement. Ask how the easement interacts with ordinary zoning and environmental approvals; one permission does not necessarily satisfy another.

## Understand continuing stewardship

Identify monitoring access, notice requirements, maintenance obligations, reporting, approval procedures and enforcement provisions. Discuss unresolved violations before purchase. Ask whether the holder has records of past inspections or concerns and how any agreed corrective work will be documented.

**Hypothetical example:** a buyer wants a farm with two family homes and a future event venue. The easement may protect a working landscape while allowing only a specified residential envelope and restricting commercial events. The property could be excellent for one household’s farm and unsuitable for that buyer’s larger plan. A discounted price does not change the permitted uses.

## Keep tax claims separate from purchase value

An existing conservation easement does not automatically give its purchaser a new charitable deduction. Proposed donations also require careful qualification, valuation and documentation. IRS Publication 526 describes requirements for qualified conservation contributions, including relevant qualified-interest and conservation-purpose conditions. Review the current rules with tax counsel for the actual transaction. [IRS charitable-contribution guidance](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526).

Compare encumbered properties using the rights they retain. Do not value a prohibited subdivision as an available exit or add hypothetical tax benefits to make a purchase look economical. Account for stewardship expenses and any effect on financing and resale flexibility.
