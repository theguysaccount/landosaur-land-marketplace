# Separate farm business income from land income

Productive acreage can support an agricultural business, a lease to an operator or a combination of uses. Those arrangements produce different cash flows and require different work. Before valuing a farm or pasture, decide whether you are underwriting the landowner’s income, the operator’s business or both.

## Start with the actual productive unit

Identify farmable or grazable acres, water availability, access, field shape, drainage, soils, fencing and improvements. Separate gross deed acreage from the area used in the production budget. Verify who owns the equipment or improvements and which leases or occupancy rights continue after a purchase.

Iowa State Extension’s land-purchase framework builds expected net income from production revenue less operating and landownership costs, with management also considered. That is a useful structure for asking what the land can support, while local yield, cost and lease assumptions still require their own evidence. [Iowa State economic analysis](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/Agdm/wholefarm/html/c2-76.html).

## Keep the two budgets distinct

For an owner leasing land, model rent actually collectible, vacancy or nonpayment, property taxes, insurance, management and owner-paid repairs or improvements. Read the lease for responsibility, timing, renewal, permitted activities and termination. Advertised regional rent is a comparison point, not a signed tenant commitment.

For an owner-operator, build the enterprise budget first: expected production, selling price, inputs, labor, equipment, marketing and overhead. Show compensation for your own time and equipment use. Then identify what remains available to support the land and financing. Avoid counting the same rent or operating margin in both budgets.

## Pasture requires a forage and water plan

Do not assume a fixed number of animals per acre across locations or seasons. Colorado State Extension’s stocking-rate tool is explicitly exploratory and points users toward local assistance when needed. Use site-specific forage, animal demand, grazing duration and management assumptions, including a response to drought or poor growth. [CSU stocking-rate estimation](https://rangemanagement.extension.colostate.edu/online-calculators/stocking-rate-estimation-cattle/).

Price the supporting system: fences, water distribution, handling, access, supplemental feed and maintenance. A parcel with grass visible in a photograph has not yet demonstrated the carrying capacity or operating cost of the proposed herd.

## A hypothetical rental calculation

Suppose a fictional property has 60 leased acres at $180 per acre annually. Gross rent is $10,800. If owner-paid taxes, insurance, management and ordinary maintenance total $3,800, the resulting $7,000 is before debt service, major capital work and income taxes. It is not $10,800 of disposable profit, and the assumed rent is not a market recommendation.

Run a second case for lower rent, a missed season or a required fence replacement. If comparing a purchase price with income, keep the capitalization assumptions explicit and avoid presenting one calculation as a guaranteed return.

Use [analysis](/analyze) for the separate owner and operator cases. Scope agronomic, grazing or lease review through [services](/services). A credible agricultural purchase rests on evidence of usable production, a realistic operating arrangement and costs assigned to the party that will actually pay them.
