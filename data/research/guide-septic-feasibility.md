# Prove septic feasibility before choosing the homesite

If a property will use onsite wastewater treatment, septic feasibility belongs near the beginning of the buying process. A pleasant building location can become unusable when the wastewater system, replacement area, well and access must fit together. Treat “perc tested” as the beginning of a document request, not a completed approval.

## Establish what was actually evaluated

Ask for the original site evaluation, test locations, date, proposed design, capacity and any issued permit. Confirm that they refer to the exact parcel and project. An old result for a neighboring lot or a smaller house may not answer your question.

EPA explains that system selection depends on factors including soil, slope, lot size, occupancy and local rules. Permitting for individual systems is principally handled at state, tribal and local levels. Ask the responsible authority what evaluations are required, whether existing work is still usable and who is qualified to perform additional work. [EPA septic questions](https://www.epa.gov/septic/frequent-questions-septic-systems).

## Draw the complete system

Have the designer show the proposed building, wastewater components, drainfield, any required replacement area, water supply, setbacks, drainage and access for maintenance. Ask whether the proposed bedroom count or future addition changes capacity. Reserve area is not automatically a convenient spot for a workshop or driveway.

Request a written explanation of the viable design options and the assumptions behind them. Identify any seasonal observation, additional testing or engineering required before the authority can decide. A contractor’s willingness to install a system is different from a permit for that design.

## Compare lifetime commitments

An initial quote should separate evaluation, design, permitting, installation, power, alarms, service agreements and recurring inspections. Include difficult access, rock excavation or other exclusions. If a more complex design is necessary, ask about maintenance availability and the consequences of service interruption.

EPA’s care guidance emphasizes inspection, pumping, water use and protecting the drainfield. That ongoing responsibility should influence both the site layout and annual ownership budget. Use the designer’s and authority’s requirements for the actual system rather than applying a generic maintenance interval blindly. [EPA septic maintenance](https://www.epa.gov/septic/how-care-your-septic-system).

## A hypothetical decision

Imagine two similar homesites. One has an approved design suited to the proposed house and a current installation quote. The other has a lower asking price and an old note saying “passed perc,” with no layout or permit. The discount is not yet a saving. First price the evaluation needed to establish an acceptable system, then compare complete acquisition and site costs.

Before your review period ends, obtain an answer to three questions: Is an approvable design plausible for this project? What evidence and approvals remain? Can you afford and maintain the likely solution? If the answer depends on future testing, keep that dependency visible in the purchase decision.
