# Read the title exceptions with the site plan

Title review is most useful when the documents are connected to what you intend to do on the land. A commitment can identify an easement or restriction, but its practical effect may become obvious only when it is placed beside the driveway, building area or future subdivision plan.

## Understand the document you received

Ask whether you are reviewing a preliminary report, a commitment to issue insurance, an issued policy or another title product. Identify the proposed insured party, property description, interest and amount. Requirements that must be satisfied before a policy is issued are different from exceptions that remain outside coverage.

Texas’s title-insurance procedural rules describe a commitment as an offer to issue a policy subject to stated terms, requirements and exceptions. Other states and products differ; use the actual transaction documents and local advice to understand your coverage. [Texas title-insurance procedural rules](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/titlem4a.html).

## Retrieve the underlying instruments

An exception list is an index for further reading. Obtain the full documents referenced by recording number and confirm they apply to the offered property. Ask qualified counsel to explain unclear language, amendments, priority or enforceability rather than relying on a listing summary.

For each easement or restriction, make a short record: who benefits, what area is affected, what activities are allowed or restricted, who maintains improvements and whether notice or consent is required. A route serving a neighbor, a utility corridor and a conservation restriction create different questions. Avoid compressing all of them into a single “easement present” badge.

## Connect coverage and site conditions

The Texas Department of Insurance advises comparing the policy’s legal description with the survey and explains that policy exceptions and additional coverage matter. Its guidance illustrates why insurance should not be treated as a promise that every boundary or use dispute is covered. [Texas title-insurance FAQ](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/title/titlefaqs.html).

Ask the surveyor to depict relevant recorded interests when the scope permits, and ask the insurer which survey findings affect coverage. Keep unresolved off-record occupation, leases or access claims visible for professional review. Title insurance, a survey and planning approval answer different questions and should inform one another.

## A hypothetical conflict

Imagine the proposed homesite overlaps a recorded utility easement. The corridor may be empty today, yet the instrument could allow future work incompatible with your layout. The right next step is to review the actual document and obtain the necessary professional and utility responses. A seller’s statement that “nothing has ever happened there” does not resolve the future-use issue.

## Close the loop before closing

Track every required release, correction, consent and final document. Ask who verifies completion and whether updated evidence will be available before funds are committed. Reconcile the final conveyed interest, legal description and policy with the deal you approved, then retain recorded copies and the issued policy.

Use [services](/services) to coordinate the title, legal and survey assignments. Update the [analysis](/analyze) if restrictions reduce the feasible use or create additional costs. A completed review should explain both what is supported and which conditions you are knowingly accepting.
