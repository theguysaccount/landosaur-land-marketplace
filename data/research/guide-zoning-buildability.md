# Is the parcel actually buildable?

“Residential zoning” is an incomplete answer to whether you can build the home you have in mind. Buildability depends on the exact parcel, the proposed project, applicable rules, site conditions and required approvals. Your task is to turn a broad label into a documented path for a specific layout.

## Begin with the correct authority and parcel

Confirm the jurisdiction, parcel identifier and legal description. Municipal boundaries, service districts and mailing addresses may not align. Ask which planning and permitting offices control the proposed work and which other agencies must review it.

Check whether the property is a legally created lot. As one jurisdiction-specific example, King County distinguishes a tax parcel from a legal lot and explains that legal-lot status does not eliminate other zoning, health and site requirements. Do not transfer that county’s procedure to another location; use it to frame the equivalent local question. [King County proof of legal lot](https://cdn.kingcounty.gov/-/media/king-county/depts/local-services/permits/building-land-use-permits/l/legal-lot.pdf?hash=C8AB26AC7DE7FDD94CB20ED5FEB6811C&rev=880c254134224462b902c1ed5a790e7a).

## Describe the project precisely

Replace “Can I build?” with a short written description: dwelling type, approximate size, bedroom count, accessory buildings, intended occupancy, access, water and wastewater plans. Include any rental, commercial, livestock or subdivision use. Different activities on the same parcel may need different permissions.

Request the current zoning designation and applicable text, then check overlays, setbacks, lot coverage, height, density and site-specific conditions. King County’s own guidance shows how base zoning can coexist with special development conditions and critical-area restrictions. It is an illustration of layered review, not a national list of rules. [King County zoning guidance](https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/local-services/buildings-property/local-services-permit-guide/permit-guide/zoning).

Private restrictions belong in a separate review. A public permit does not itself resolve restrictions in recorded covenants, easements or agreements.

## Make the constraints meet on one drawing

Have the relevant professionals place the building, driveway, water supply, wastewater system, replacement area, drainage and applicable setbacks on the same preliminary site plan. A parcel can appear to satisfy each requirement separately but fail when they must fit together.

**Hypothetical example:** a lot allows a dwelling in principle, but the preferred building area overlaps the only plausible wastewater area. Moving the house may require a longer driveway and more grading. The decision is whether a compliant, affordable alternative layout exists—not whether the zoning label sounds favorable.

## Preserve the approval path

For every unresolved item, record the authority, application, supporting study, expected sequence and decision date. Ask whether a response is informal guidance, a formal determination or an issued permit, and whether it expires or depends on the exact plans submitted. Keep dated responses with the questions that produced them.

Use [services](/services) to scope a planning or site-feasibility review. Put its costs and dependencies into [analysis](/analyze). Make a purchase decision around a project whose remaining approvals you understand, with contract review periods and exit options addressed by your advisers before deadlines become binding.
